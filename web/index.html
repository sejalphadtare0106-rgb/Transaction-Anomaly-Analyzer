Method

Five rules, fixed points

The customer baseline uses that customer's successful transactions.

Detection rules, conditions and points
RuleConditionPoints
High amountAmount is more than 3× the customer's median successful amount and above INR 5,00030
High frequencyThe row sits in a window of 5 or more transactions by the same customer within 60 minutes20
Unusual cityCity is not the customer's home city and is under 5% of their successful transactions25
Night timingTransaction is 00:00–04:59 and under 10% of the customer's successful transactions happen then15
Repeated failuresThe failed row sits in a window of 3 or more failed attempts within 30 minutes20

Score: sum of points for rules that fired, capped at 100.

Levels: None 0, Low 1–29, Medium 30–59, High 60+.

Not used: no machine learning, no black-box model, no external data.

Limits

What this does not prove

  • The data is synthetic. It is not real bank data and does not contain real customer behaviour.
  • The validation is circular. I planted the anomalies, so the metrics show recovery of planted patterns.
  • The rules are simple and fixed. They cannot learn new patterns.
  • Risk scores are illustrative rules-based scores, not validated fraud probabilities.
  • A flag is not proof of fraud. It means a person should review the transaction.
  • Normal large purchases and normal travel create false positives, especially for the high-amount and unusual-city rules.
  • The INR 5,000 floor causes false negatives for low-spend customers.
  • This is a portfolio demo on 10,000 rows, not a production fraud system.
